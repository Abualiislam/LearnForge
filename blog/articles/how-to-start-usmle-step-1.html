How to Start USMLE Step 1 Preparation

LearnForge • 2026

Starting USMLE Step 1 preparation can feel overwhelming because the exam covers a large amount of foundational medical science. A better approach is to build a structured system instead of trying to study everything at once.

1. Understand What You Need to Learn

Begin by identifying the major disciplines and organ-system topics you need to cover. Create a checklist and use it to track progress rather than relying on memory.

2. Choose a Small Core Resource Set

Avoid collecting too many resources. A practical system usually has one primary content resource, a question bank, a review source and a spaced-repetition system if you find flashcards useful.

Study principle: A resource is valuable when you actively use it. More resources do not automatically produce better preparation.

3. Start With Questions Early

Practice questions help reveal what you understand and what you only recognize. Reviewing the explanation after a question is often more valuable than simply recording whether the answer was correct.

4. Use Active Recall

Close the book and try to explain a concept from memory. Draw pathways, explain mechanisms aloud and answer questions without looking at the answer first.

5. Use Spaced Repetition

Important facts should be reviewed at increasing intervals. The exact schedule can vary, but the central idea is to revisit information before it is completely forgotten.

6. Track Your Progress

Create a simple table containing topics, study status, question performance and weak areas.

  • Not started
  • Learning
  • Reviewed
  • Strong

7. Protect Your Study Time

A realistic schedule that you can maintain is better than an extreme schedule that lasts only a few days. Set daily priorities and leave time for review.

Conclusion

A successful Step 1 preparation system is built around consistency, active learning, practice questions and continuous identification of weak areas. Start small, measure progress and improve your system as you learn.
